Getting started

Affiliate marketing, explained in 5 minutes

5 min read

What affiliate marketing is, how you actually get paid, what you need to start with $0, and the honest part nobody posts about.

You already recommend things. A tool that saves you time, an app you can't live without. Affiliate marketing just means you recommend it with your own link, and when someone signs up or buys through that link, the company pays you a share.

The buyer pays the same price. The company pays you out of its marketing budget, because you brought them a customer.

How the money works

There are two kinds of commission, and the difference matters a lot:

  • One-time: you get paid once per sale. Common for hosting, travel bookings and courses.
  • Recurring: you get a share of every monthly payment, usually for 12 months, sometimes for as long as the customer stays. Common for software (SaaS) and AI tools.
💡
Example (simple maths, not a promise): a tool costs $20 a month and pays 30% recurring for 12 months. One customer = $6 a month, up to $72 in a year. Ten customers who stay = up to $720. That's why software is popular with affiliates: one good guide can keep paying.

What you need to start (it can be $0)

  1. A topic. Something you can talk about for a year, e.g. AI tools for small shops, content for tutors, tools for travel creators.
  2. A place people find you. Threads, Instagram, TikTok, a newsletter.
  3. Something that helps. A short guide, a checklist, a before/after. People buy from the person who solved their problem.
  4. The links. Apply to the affiliate programs of tools you actually use. Search "[tool name] affiliate program". Most are free to join.

Some programs approve anyone. Others want to see a website or an audience first, which is one reason to keep your helpful posts in one place, like a simple site.

The honest part

  • It's slow at first. Most people earn little in their first months. It grows as your helpful content piles up.
  • Programs change. Companies cut rates or close programs. Don't depend on just one.
  • Trust is everything. Recommend only what you'd tell a friend to use. One pushy post can cost you more than a commission.
⚠️
If someone promises "passive income in 30 days" or shows big income screenshots to sell you a course, be careful. Real affiliate income is built, not switched on.

Always say it's an affiliate link

In most countries you must tell people when you earn from a link, and it must be clear, near the link, in the same language as your post. Writing just "affiliate link" is often not enough. A simple version:

Disclosure line 
I earn a commission if you buy through this link, at no extra cost to you.
