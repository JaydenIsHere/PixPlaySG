Getting started

Why affiliate marketing is worth taking seriously (and what it's not)

6 min read

No product to build, almost no cost to start, and skills that help any business. Plus the honest parts: it's slow at first, and it's not passive at the start.

Say "affiliate marketing" and many people picture spammy links or someone selling a get-rich-quick course. That reputation is earned by the people doing it badly. Done properly, it's one of the simplest small businesses you can start, and one of the most misunderstood.

Why it's worth your time

  1. No product to build. No stock, no shipping, no customer support. The company handles all of that. Your job is to help people choose well.
  2. Almost no cost to start. A social account and free tools are enough. Your main investment is time.
  3. Software pays again and again. Many AI and software tools pay you a share of every monthly payment, often for 12 months or more. Helpful content you wrote months ago can keep earning.
  4. The skills carry over. Writing clearly, understanding what people need, making content with AI: useful for your own business, your job, or anything you start next.
  5. It fits around your life. You can do it next to a job, from anywhere with an internet connection, at your own pace.

What it's not

  • Not passive at the start. It becomes more passive later, once you have helpful content that keeps getting found. At first it's work.
  • Not quick. Most people earn little in their first months. Plan in months, not weeks.
  • Not posting links everywhere. Links with no help around them get ignored, and can get your account limited.
  • Not guaranteed. Companies change their commission rates and close programs. Spread across a few tools.
⚠️
If someone shows you big income screenshots and promises the same for you, they're usually selling a course, not teaching a skill.
